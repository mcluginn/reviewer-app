Module 1: ODE vs. PDE Classification

Core Definition

Start by identifying the unknown function and its independent variables. The number of derivative terms does not decide whether an equation is ordinary or partial; the deciding factor is whether differentiation is with respect to one independent variable or several.

Ordinary DE (ODE)

Uses ordinary derivatives with respect to one independent variable. It may contain several dependent variables.

\( \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + 3\frac{dy}{dx} + 2y = 0 \)

Only \( x \) is independent, so this is an ODE.

Partial DE (PDE)

Uses partial derivatives because the unknown function depends on two or more independent variables.

\( \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2} = c^2 \left(\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2}\right) \)

The function \( u \) depends on position \( x \) and time \( t \).

Classification checklist

  1. Identify the dependent variable, such as \( y \) or \( u \).
  2. List the independent variables on which it depends.
  3. Inspect the derivative notation: d for ordinary derivatives and ∂ for partial derivatives.
  4. After classifying ODE or PDE, find the order from the highest derivative.
\( \frac{dx}{dt} = y, \quad \frac{dy}{dt} = -x \)

A system of ODEs: both unknowns vary only with \( t \).

\( \frac{\partial^3 u}{\partial x^2 \partial t} + u = 0 \)

A third-order PDE: the mixed derivative has total order 2 + 1 = 3.

Module 2: Order and Degree

Identification Rules
Order: The order of the highest derivative present in the equation.
Degree: The exponent of the highest-order derivative after the equation is written as a polynomial in its derivatives. If no such polynomial form exists, the degree is undefined.

Discussion: order first, degree second

Order and degree are not the same. First locate the highest derivative to obtain the order. Then—only if the equation is polynomial in all derivatives—read the exponent attached to that highest-order derivative. A larger exponent on a lower derivative does not control the degree.

\( y''' + (y')^5 + y = 0 \)

Order 3, degree 1. The power 5 is on a lower derivative.

\( y'' + \sin(y') = 0 \)

Order 2, degree undefined. The equation is not polynomial in its derivatives.

Radical example: \( \sqrt{y''} + y = 0 \) can be written as \( y'' = y^2 \) after isolating and squaring the radical. In the usual textbook classification, its order is 2 and degree is 1.

Module 3: The 3 Rules of Linearity

Crucial Topic

An nth-order ODE is linear in y if it can be written in the standard form below, where every coefficient depends only on the independent variable x.

\( a_n(x)y^{(n)} + a_{n-1}(x)y^{(n-1)} + \cdots + a_1(x)y' + a_0(x)y = g(x) \)

The equation may be nonhomogeneous: \( g(x) \) does not need to equal zero.

✓ 1
Power of 1: Dependent variable \( y \) and its derivatives are raised ONLY to the 1st power. (No \( y^2 \), \( (y')^3 \)).
✓ 2
No Products: No multiplication between \( y \) and its derivatives (No \( y \cdot y' \), \( y' \cdot y'' \)).
✓ 3
No nonlinear functions of y: Terms such as \( \sin(y) \), \( e^y \), \( \ln(y) \), or \( \frac{1}{y} \) are not allowed. Functions of \( x \) such as \( \sin(x) \) and \( e^x \) are allowed.

Discussion: inspect y, not the appearance of x

Linearity concerns how the dependent variable and its derivatives appear. Coefficients may be complicated functions of x. The equation becomes nonlinear when a y-term is powered, multiplied by another y-term, placed in a nonlinear function, or used as a coefficient of another derivative.

\( (1 + x^2)y'' - 3xy' + 4y = e^x \)

Linear: \( y \), \( y' \), and \( y'' \) appear only to the first power and are not multiplied together.

\( y y' + x = 0 \)

Nonlinear: \( y \) multiplies \( y' \).

\( (y'')^2 + y = 0 \)

Nonlinear: \( y'' \) has power 2.

Common error: \( x^2y' \) is linear because \( x^2 \) is a coefficient depending only on \( x \). In contrast, \( y^2y' \) is nonlinear.

Module 4: Solutions, IVPs & Applications

Methods & Models

Verifying a proposed solution

Differentiate the proposed function as needed, substitute both the function and its derivatives into the differential equation, and confirm that both sides agree on the stated domain.

\( \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{4y}{x}, \quad y = Cx^4, \quad x \neq 0 \)

Since \( y' = 4Cx^3 \) and \( \frac{4y}{x} = \frac{4(Cx^4)}{x} = 4Cx^3 \), the family satisfies the ODE.

Initial Value Problem (IVP) Condition Rule

A standard nth-order ODE normally needs n independent initial conditions at the same point \( x_0 \) to determine the n constants in its general solution.

2nd-order IVP → \( y(x_0) = y_0 \) and \( y'(x_0) = y_1 \)

These conditions determine a unique local solution only when the relevant existence-and-uniqueness conditions are satisfied.

From a general solution to a particular solution

For \( y' = 2x \), integrate to obtain \( y = x^2 + C \). Apply \( y(0) = 3 \): \( 3 = 0^2 + C \), so \( C = 3 \) and the particular solution is \( y = x^2 + 3 \).

Radioactive Decay Quick-Formula

The statement “rate of decay is proportional to the amount present” becomes \( \frac{dN}{dt} = -kN \) with \( k > 0 \). Separation and integration give the exponential model.

\( N(t) = N_0e^{-kt} = N_0(0.5)^{t/T_{\text{half}}} \)

The equivalent parameters satisfy \( T_{\text{half}} = \frac{\ln(2)}{k} \). Check a model by confirming \( N(0) = N_0 \) and testing one known data point.

Orthogonal Trajectories Workflow

  1. Differentiate given family of curves and eliminate constant \( C \) to find \( \frac{dy}{dx} \).
  2. Replace \( \frac{dy}{dx} \) with negative reciprocal: \( \left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)_{\text{ortho}} = -\frac{1}{\frac{dy}{dx}} \).
  3. Separate variables and integrate to get the orthogonal family equation.
\( y = kx^2 \) gives \( y' = \frac{2y}{x} \). The perpendicular slope is \( -\frac{x}{2y} \), so \( 2y \, dy = -x \, dx \) and \( x^2 + 2y^2 = C \).

At regular intersection points, the two tangent slopes multiply to −1.
